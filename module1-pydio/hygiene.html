Password Hygiene

The cornerstone of organizational cybersecurity

Learning Objectives
  • Understand the critical role passwords play in organizational cybersecurity.
  • Recognize common threats related to weak, reused, or compromised passwords.
  • Create and manage strong, unique passwords across all systems.
  • Apply multi-factor authentication (MFA) effectively.
  • Understand how password hygiene impacts both personal and organizational data security.

Introduction

Passwords remain the first line of defense against cyberattacks, yet they are often one of the weakest points in organizational security.

Why Password Hygiene Matters

  • Data Breaches: Weak passwords are a leading cause of data breaches.
  • Credential Stuffing: Stolen passwords are reused across systems.
  • Insider Threats: Shared or poorly managed passwords are dangerous.
  • Regulatory Compliance: Many industries require strict password policies.

Common Password Risks

  • Weak passwords (e.g., “123456”).
  • Reused passwords across accounts.
  • Sharing or writing passwords down.
  • Not changing passwords after compromise.

Best Practices for Defence

  • Use long, strong passwords or passphrases.
  • Use a password manager for unique credentials.
  • Enable Multi-Factor Authentication (MFA).
  • Never share passwords.
  • Update after any suspected compromise.
  • Monitor alerts and respond quickly.

Conclusion

Strong password hygiene reduces the risk of compromise. Combining secure passwords with MFA and good practices creates a strong defense against credential-based attacks.
